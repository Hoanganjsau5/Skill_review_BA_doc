## 1. Relevant Scope for `re.requirement-quality`

### Clause 5.2.5 — Characteristics of Individual Requirements
ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 distinguishes between the quality characteristics of **an individual requirement statement** (Clause 5.2.5) and the characteristics of **a set of requirements** (Clause 5.2.6).

The nine characteristics of an individual requirement are:
1. **Necessary:** The requirement defines an essential capability, characteristic, constraint, or quality factor that fulfills an identifiable need (business, stakeholder, or parent requirement); if removed, a deficiency remains that cannot be satisfied by other capabilities.
2. **Appropriate:** The specific intent and level of detail of the requirement are appropriate to the level of the entity to which it refers (avoiding unnecessary design/implementation constraints at a given abstraction level, while allowing genuine upper-level constraints when justified).
3. **Unambiguous:** The requirement is stated such that it can be interpreted in only one clear way by its intended readers.
4. **Complete:** The requirement sufficiently describes the necessary capability, characteristic, constraint, or quality factor without needing other amplification to understand what is required at its level.
5. **Singular:** The requirement states a single capability, characteristic, constraint, or quality factor.
6. **Feasible:** The requirement can be realized within system constraints (e.g., cost, schedule, technical, operational) with acceptable risk.
7. **Verifiable:** The requirement is structured and worded such that its realization can be proven to the customer's satisfaction at the level the requirement exists (via inspection, analysis, demonstration, or test).
8. **Correct:** The requirement is an accurate representation of the need from which it was transformed (authoritative source, stakeholder intent, or parent requirement).
9. **Conforming:** The individual items conform to an approved standard template and style for writing requirements, when applicable.

### Clause 5.2.6 — Characteristics of a Set of Requirements (`re.requirement-set-quality`)
Characteristics of a requirement set belong to `re.requirement-set-quality` (distinct from individual requirement statement quality in Clause 5.2.5):
1. **Complete:** The requirement set stands alone such that it sufficiently describes the necessary capabilities, characteristics, constraints, or quality factors to meet the project's declared scope without missing companion requirements or unexplained gaps.
2. **Consistent:** The set contains individual requirements that are unique, do not conflict with or overlap one another in the set, and use homogeneous units, terminology, and definitions throughout.
3. **Affordable / Feasible (Set-Level):** The complete set of requirements can be satisfied by a solution that is obtainable/achievable within life cycle constraints (note: deep cross-constraint/architectural trade-offs delegate to `fr.requirement-conflict-analysis`).
4. **Bounded:** The set of requirements maintains the identified scope for the solution needed to meet stakeholder needs without unplaced or out-of-bound drift inside the set structure.

### Clause 6.4 — Requirements Validation (`re.requirement-validation`)
Requirements validation confirms that the specified requirements sufficiently and accurately represent the stakeholder needs, business objectives, and operational concepts:
- Evaluates whether the requirements are the *right requirements* for stakeholders and system objectives.
- Identifies missing or only partially addressed stakeholder needs, distortion of stakeholder intent during transformation, misalignment with business/system KPIs or authoritative policies, and unjustified requirements (gold-plating).

### Clause 6.5 — Requirements Traceability (`re.requirement-traceability`)
Requirements traceability establishes and maintains relationships between requirements and related life cycle elements:
- **Backward Traceability:** Links each requirement to its source (business objective, stakeholder need, parent requirement, regulatory source).
- **Forward Traceability:** Links each requirement to downstream artifacts derived from or verifying it (child requirements, Use Cases, User Stories, Acceptance Criteria, architecture/design elements, Test Cases).
- **Bidirectional Consistency & Relationship Integrity:** Ensures forward and backward links are mutually consistent, resolve to valid non-deprecated IDs, and reflect genuine structural derivation/allocation/verification relationships.
